Key findings

Early tenure is the single biggest risk window. Attrition among employees with under a year of tenure is 34.9% — more than 4x the rate for employees past 10 years (8.1%). Attrition drops sharply and steadily with every additional year, which points to an onboarding and early-engagement gap rather than a company-wide retention problem.
Overtime nearly triples attrition risk. Employees logging overtime leave at 30.5%, versus 10.4% for those who don't — the strongest single predictor in the dataset after tenure. This is a workload/burnout signal, not a compensation one, since it holds even after accounting for pay.
Sales Representatives are a distinct outlier. At 39.8% attrition, this single role is nearly 2.5x the company average and far above every other role, including other Sales positions (Sales Executive: 17.5%). This looks like a role-specific issue — likely quota pressure, ramp difficulty, or compensation structure — rather than a department-wide Sales problem.
Pay matters most at the bottom, not across the board. Attrition falls sharply between the lowest income quartile (29.3%) and the second (14.2%), but barely moves between Q2, Q3, and Q4. This suggests retention pay strategy should target the bottom pay band specifically rather than broad-based raises.
03

Recommendations

  1. Introduce a structured 90-day early-tenure program. Given 34.9% first-year attrition, add manager check-ins at day 30/60/90 with a short pulse survey, rather than relying on annual reviews to catch disengagement.
  2. Audit overtime load in high-risk roles first. Start with Sales Representative and Laboratory Technician roles, where both overtime exposure and attrition are highest — cap sustained overtime or add temporary staffing support during peak periods.
  3. Run a targeted diagnostic on the Sales Representative role. Its attrition rate is an outlier even within its own department — a short exit-interview review or stay-interview round with current reps would clarify whether the driver is quota design, ramp time, or manager support.
  4. Set a minimum pay review trigger for the bottom income quartile. Rather than company-wide raises, prioritize compensation review for employees in Q1, where the retention return on pay adjustment is clearly highest.
